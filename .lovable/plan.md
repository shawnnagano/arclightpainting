## Why Asbestos Comes Before Paint

Asbestos is a mineral fiber that was used in many building products for years. The U.S. Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) lists textured paint and patching compounds used on walls and ceilings among the places asbestos may be found.

The EPA says exposure may occur only when asbestos-containing material is disturbed or damaged in a way that releases particles and fibers into the air. Materials may release fibers when they are disturbed, damaged, removed improperly, repaired, cut, torn, sanded, sawed, drilled or scraped. Several of those are normal steps in painting prep.

The EPA names three major health effects linked to asbestos exposure: lung cancer; mesothelioma, a rare cancer of the thin lining of the lung, chest, abdomen and heart; and asbestosis, a serious, progressive, long-term lung disease.

The EPA also says you generally cannot tell whether a material contains asbestos just by looking at it, unless it is labeled. And if building materials in your home are not damaged and will not be disturbed, you do not need to have your home tested.

## Step 1: Testing With a Good Faith Survey

Washington State's Department of Labor & Industries (L&I) says that before any construction, renovation, remodeling, maintenance, repair or demolition project, homeowners, building owners and contractors must have a good faith inspection for asbestos on the materials that could be disturbed. L&I says the inspection must be done by an accredited inspector, such as an AHERA Building Inspector.

This is where Asbestos Removal of Puget Sound starts. According to its [testing page](https://asbestosremovalofpugetsound.com/testing/):

- **An AHERA-certified inspector** visits the site to identify suspect materials.
- **Samples are taken** of the materials that matter, including flooring, mastics and joint compounds.
- **Samples go to partner labs** for Polarized Light Microscopy (PLM) analysis.
- **Lead testing is available** by XRF or bulk sampling, which can matter in older homes before a remodel.
- **You receive a permit-ready PDF report** with lab results, photo logs and clear conclusions and recommendations.

Share that report with us when you request your quote. L&I says work should not start until a copy of the inspection results has been provided.

## What Washington Requires

A few more L&I points every Seattle homeowner should know:

- **Both sides can be cited.** If a good faith inspection is not provided before work starts, L&I says both the contractor and the property owner may be cited.
- **Fines add up.** L&I lists a fine of $600 a day for work done without an AHERA Building Inspector testing for asbestos, or for not sharing the results with contractors and workers who could be exposed.
- **The homeowner exception is narrow.** L&I says if you do the work yourself in your own home that is not used for commercial purposes, you are exempt from the general survey requirements. But once you bring in contractors, you must still give them the relevant information.

## What Not to Do

The EPA's guidance is clear:

- **Do not saw, sand, scrape or drill** into materials that may contain asbestos.
- **Do not dust, sweep or vacuum** debris that may contain asbestos.
- **Do leave undamaged asbestos-containing materials alone.**

The EPA also says homeowners should make sure the workers they hire to handle asbestos are trained and accredited.
